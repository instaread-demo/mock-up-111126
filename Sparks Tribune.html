Endangered Eating: Literary Conversations with Culinary Historian Sarah Lohman

RENO, NV —  Nevada Humanities is presenting two upcoming public programs featuring author and culinary historian Sarah Lohman. The first program will take place on October 19, 2026, in Reno, NV, and the second program will take place on November 12, 2026, in Henderson, NV. 

Sarah Lohman is a culinary historian and the author of the bestselling books Endangered Eating: America’s Vanishing Foods and Eight Flavors: The Untold Story of American Cuisine. Nevada Humanities selected Nevada author Sarah Lohman and her book Endangered Eating: America’s Vanishing Foods to represent Nevada at the 2026 Library of Congress National Book Festival in Washington, D.C. Her book is part of the Library of Congress Center for the Book’s national Great Reads from Great Places initiative, a list which includes recommended books for youth and adults from each affiliate center from the 50 states and six U.S. jurisdictions, including the Nevada Center for the Book, a program of Nevada Humanities that is one of 56 affiliates of the Library of Congress and its Center for the Book. 

“Nevada Humanities is thrilled to host two evenings of exciting conversations with culinary historian Sarah Lohman,” said Christina Barr, executive director of Nevada Humanities. “For over two decades, Sarah Lohman has explored American food and foodways with thoughtful consideration and exciting adventures that have touched every corner of our nation. Her work is grounded in the communities that she encounters – writing about preserving local culinary traditions and rare foods, and preparing dishes and recipes. She has also explored the history and traditions of Nevada foodways, particularly in Las Vegas. These two programs with Sarah Lohman are not to be missed.”

On Monday, October 19, 2026, Nevada Humanities, in partnership with the Reynolds School of Journalism at the University of Nevada, Reno, is presenting a conversation with culinary historian Sarah Lohman and author and journalist Alan Deutschman from 6:00 pm to 7:00 pm at the Wells Fargo Auditorium, Mathewson-IGT Knowledge Center, on the University of Nevada, Reno campus (1664 North Virginia Street, Reno, NV, 89557). This event is free and open to the public. A book signing will follow the conversation. Registration is required; learn more and register on the Nevada Humanities website at nevadahumanities.org.

Lohman and Deutschman will discuss Lohman’s exploration of the history of food in Nevada and beyond, as well as her book Endangered Eating: America’s Vanishing Foods. Their conversation will touch on how Lohman uses food to explore different communities and how these communities are shaped by their culinary landscapes, and the often surprising ways that our own food practices and ingredients are rooted in social and political histories. 

This October event is produced by Nevada Humanities and made possible with support from the Reynolds School of Journalism at the University of Nevada, Reno, the Federation of State Humanities Councils with support from the Mellon Foundation, the National Endowment for the Humanities, the Institute of Museum and Library Services and Chief Officers of State Library Agencies, and individual donors. This event is also part of the national initiative By the People: Conversations Beyond 250, which is a series of community-driven programs created by humanities councils in collaboration with local partners. By the People was developed by the Federation of State Humanities Councils and the Smithsonian Center for Folklife and Cultural Heritage. 

On Thursday, November 12, 2026, Nevada Humanities will host another evening event with Sarah Lohman in conversation with Nevada’s 2024 Great Reads from Great Places author Kim Foster from 6:00 to 7:30 pm in the auditorium of the Kasner Academic Building in Henderson (1300 Nevada State Drive, Henderson, NV, 89002). A public reception will take place at 6:00 pm, with the conversation scheduled from 6:30 – 7:30 pm. This event is free and open to the public. 

This November event is presented by Nevada Humanities, the Marydean Martin Writers’ Project, a program of the Office for the Arts at Nevada State University, and the City of Henderson. This event is made possible by support from the Federation of State Humanities Councils with support from the Mellon Foundation, the National Endowment for the Humanities, the Institute of Museum and Library Services and Chief Officers of State Library Agencies, and individual donors. This event is also part of the national initiative By the People: Conversations Beyond 250, which is a series of community-driven programs created by humanities councils in collaboration with local partners. By the People was developed by the Federation of State Humanities Councils and the Smithsonian Center for Folklife and Cultural Heritage. 

Sarah Lohman is a culinary historian and the author of the bestselling books Endangered Eating: America’s Vanishing Foods and Eight Flavors: The Untold Story of American Cuisine. She focuses on the history of food as a way to access the stories of diverse Americans. Endangered Eating was a New York Times Editors’ Choice, and was named one of the Best Books of 2023 by Amazon’s Editors, Food & Wine, and Adam Gopnik on the Milk Street podcast. It was a finalist for the Nach Waxman Prize for Food & Drink Scholarship and winner of the Ohioana Library Book Prize for Nonfiction. Lohman’s work has been featured in The Wall Street Journal, The New York Times, and NPR. Lohman has lectured across the country, from the Smithsonian Museum of American History in Washington, DC, to The Neon Museum in Las Vegas. Lohman is based in Henderson.

Alan Deutschman holds the Reynolds Chair of Business Journalism at the Reynolds School of Journalism, University of Nevada, Reno. In his 35-year career as a journalist, Alan Deutschman has been the Silicon Valley correspondent for Fortune, a senior writer at Fast Company, the “Profit Motive” columnist for GQ and a contributing editor at Vanity Fair and New York Magazine. Since joining the Reynolds School of Journalism in 2011, he has taught courses in magazine writing, business journalism, and food writing in Paris, Italy, and the Spanish Basque country. 

Deutschman is the author of The Second Coming of Steve Jobs, A Tale of Two Valleys, Change or Die, Walk the Walk, and How Steve Jobs Changed Our World. His books have been translated into eight languages. His articles have also been published in the New York Times Magazine, the Wall Street Journal, Wired, Conde Nast Traveler, Newsweek, Salon.com, the San Francisco Chronicle, and the Boston Globe. He has appeared as a commentator for NBC’s “Today Show,” CNBC’s “Squawk Box,” Bloomberg TV’s “Bloomberg West” and documentaries on CNN, CNBC, Bloomberg TV, and the Discovery Channel.

Kim Foster is a James Beard Award-winning writer and author of The Meth Lunches: Food and Longing in an American City. Foster writes about people enmeshed in mental illness, family separation, poverty, addiction, trauma, and incarceration. She has written for, or been written about, in publications including The Washington Post, Slate, The Guardian, and others. Foster lives in Las Vegas, Nevada. 

ABOUT NEVADA HUMANITIES

Nevada Humanities is one of 56 independent, nonprofit state and jurisdictional humanities councils that partner with the National Endowment for the Humanities. With offices in Reno and Las Vegas, Nevada Humanities creates public cultural programs and supports public cultural projects statewide that define the Nevada experience and facilitate the exploration of ideas that matter to the people of Nevada. For more information about Nevada Humanities visit nevadahumanities.org.
