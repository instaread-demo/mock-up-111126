What Is an eSIM?

Think of an eSIM as a SIM card without the tiny piece of plastic you have to physically put into your phone. Instead of arriving in Spain and looking for a store where you can purchase a local SIM, you buy your eSIM ahead of time and install it on your phone while you’re still at home.

Once you arrive in Barcelona, you can switch your mobile data over to the travel eSIM and connect to a local network. Viola!

That’s the part I really like. The setup happens before the trip, when I’m sitting comfortably at home with Wi-Fi and a functioning brain. Not after a long flight when I’m dragging a suitcase through an unfamiliar airport.

You can also leave your regular SIM in your phone, which means you don’t have to worry about removing that tiny little card and then inevitably wondering where you put it.

Why Use an eSIM Instead of Your Regular SIM or Carrier Plan?

You have a few other options for getting data abroad.

Use your regular cell phone carrier: Your carrier may offer an international roaming plan, but pricing and data allowances vary by provider and plan. You also need to make sure international service is activated before you leave.

Buy a physical SIM in Spain: This can work, but you have to find a place selling SIM cards, choose a plan, install it and keep track of your original SIM.

I’ve been traveling internationally long enough to know that the thing that sounds easy at home can suddenly feel very complicated when you’re exhausted, jet-lagged, and standing in an airport you’ve never seen before.

Or you can use an eSIM: You can purchase and install it before you leave home, keep your physical SIM in your phone and switch to the eSIM for mobile data when you arrive.

For me, that last option wins on convenience. And if you’re already a little nervous about navigating a new country, having your phone ready to go before you leave home is one less thing to worry about.

There isn’t just one way to get mobile data when you’re traveling. Your regular carrier may offer an international plan, or you can purchase a physical SIM after you arrive. Both can work, but…

Your regular carrier: Your phone company may have an international roaming option, but the cost, data allowance and terms depend on your specific carrier and plan. You’ll also need to make sure international service is activated before your trip.

A physical SIM: You can purchase a local SIM once you’re in Spain, but that means finding a retailer, choosing a plan, installing the SIM and keeping track of your original SIM while you’re traveling.

None of this is impossible. I’ve traveled enough to know, though, that I don’t always want to add another errand to the first day of a trip.

An eSIM: You purchase it before you leave, install it ahead of time and then use it for mobile data when you arrive. For me, the convenience is the biggest selling point. Even though I spent a lot of time exploring on foot, I was constantly checking directions and figuring out how to get from one part of the city to another. I used my phone for public transportation, restaurant research, attraction tickets, translations and messaging.

Having data meant I could figure those things out as I went instead of trying to plan every single detail before leaving my hotel.

Will You Actually Need Cell Data in Barcelona?

Yes. Could you technically survive without it? Of course. Barcelona existed long before Google Maps, Uber, and smartphones. But I’m not interested in making travel harder simply because I technically can.

When you’re visiting Barcelona for the first time, there is already enough to figure out. You’re navigating a new country, unfamiliar streets, a different language, transportation, restaurant reservations, attraction tickets, and the general sensory overload that comes with arriving somewhere as extraordinary as Barcelona.

You don’t need to add “Where can I find a SIM card?” to the list.

Especially if you’re an introvert like me, there is something very comforting about being able to solve a problem on your own phone rather than walking into a shop and trying to explain exactly what you need while you’re jet-lagged.

Technically? No. People traveled to Barcelona long before smartphones existed. You could download maps, print reservations and write down every address before leaving home. But I’m going to assume you don’t want to travel like it’s 1998.

Barcelona is a city where having your phone available can make the day significantly easier. There are a lot of attractions spread throughout different neighborhoods, and even when you’re walking, you’ll probably want directions at some point.

During my time in Barcelona, my phone helped me navigate between places like the Gothic Quarter, Sagrada Família, Park Güell and the other stops on my itinerary. I also used it for restaurant information, transportation, tickets, translations and messaging.

And sometimes the most useful thing your phone does is something completely unplanned. You spot a restaurant that looks good. You wonder what a building is. You need to figure out whether to walk or take the Metro. You suddenly remember that you wanted to see one more attraction.

That’s the beauty of having data. You don’t have to know everything before you leave your hotel. You can figure things out as you go.
