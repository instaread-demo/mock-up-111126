Stanford nurse wins national skills summit

Published 3:26 pm Tuesday, November 10, 2026

By Casey Roberts/Interior Journal

Jessica Carter stands beside a banner at Stanford Crossing in Stanford, Ky., recognizing her first-place finish in the nursing category at Journey’s 2026 Grand Skills Summit. (Stanford Crossing)

STANFORD — The nursing skills Jessica Carter uses every day at Stanford Crossing helped her win first place in a company competition, earning her a new car, a vacation and a scholarship.

Carter won the nursing category at Journey’s 2026 Skills Summit finale in October in Cumming, Georgia, after competing at a regional summit in Lexington in July. Journey is Stanford Crossing’s management company.

“When I won, I felt like I won the lottery,” Carter said.

Her thoughts went to her daughter, Ava, and her brother, Devin, and how proud they would be. She also looked toward her bosses, Celina and Tevis, grateful that they had believed she could win.

Carter said her prizes included a new Toyota Camry, a $10,000 vacation and a scholarship.

The competition tested nurses on wound care, tracheostomy care, starting an IV, giving an intramuscular injection and performing CPR. Carter estimated she competed against 10 to 12 other nurses at the regional event, with eight or nine nurses competing at the finale.

The procedures were familiar. Performing them in front of an audience was harder.

“The most challenging part for me was nerves and having so many people watching me,” she said.

Carter said she did not do any special studying or preparation. She relied on what she had learned during five years as a nurse and the techniques she uses on the job.

Her interest in nursing began long before she entered the profession.

Carter lived in Crab Orchard from 1996 until 2023, when she bought a house in Stanford. As a child, she accompanied her mother, a nurse, to the nursing home and got to know the residents.

“My mother was a nurse and I wanted to make her proud,” Carter said. “I used to go into the nursing home with her as a kid and that’s when I fell in love with the residents.”

She has worked as a floor nurse at Stanford Crossing since February and had also worked there previously.

For Carter, long-term care offers relationships she values beyond the daily nursing duties. She said she never really had grandparents, and the people she cares for have become like family.

Her favorite part of the work is being available when residents need someone, whether that means holding a hand, listening or reassuring them they are not alone.

She described arriving at work to find residents waiting for her, smiling, offering hugs and telling her they had missed her.

“That’s what makes this kind of work worthwhile,” Carter said.

Still, she is candid about how demanding nursing can be and the days when she feels overwhelmed.

“Being a nurse is very difficult,” she said. “It weighs on you physically, mentally, emotionally. Especially if you care so much for others.”

The recognition has given her encouragement to continue. When she questions why she keeps doing such difficult work, she said, residents’ smiles and laughter and hugs from their family members remind her of the difference she can make.

“I may not be able to change the world, but I can be a helping hand to those in need,” Carter said. “And that’s enough for me.”

Her coworkers have shared in the celebration. Carter said some have cried with excitement and sent her pictures of themselves with the sign recognizing her win. Stanford Crossing also shared a photo of Carter standing beside a banner identifying her as the first-place nurse at the 2026 Grand Skills Summit.

She credits Ava, Devin, her family, her administrators and the patients and family members she has known throughout her career for supporting her.

Her parents and daughter are proud, she said. For Ava, Carter hopes the accomplishment will be an example she can carry into her own future.

“She will know that nothing is impossible and she can achieve her aspirations and dreams because she watched her momma do it.”
