Virginia Tech Men’s Basketball Guts Out 93-88 Overtime Win Over Iowa

David Cunningham
Managing Editor
November 11, 2026 9

Sponsored by
0:00 / 9:04
Instaread

Two of Virginia Tech men’s basketball’s starters fouled out in regulation, but it still found a way to gut out a 93-88 overtime win over Iowa in Sioux City, Iowa, on Tuesday night.

Hokies (3-0) starting center Miles Heide was in foul trouble for most of the evening — heck, nearly everyone was — and picked up his fifth with 6:27 remaining in the second half. He had just one point and three rebounds in 10 minutes.

However, their most important loss came on the final play of regulation when Ben Hammond, their leading scorer with a game-high 32 points, fouled Hawkeyes (2-1) guard Tyreek Coleman with 1.4 seconds remaining.

It was Hammond’s fifth personal, and Tech only led 79-78, so Coleman had a chance to win it for Iowa at the stripe. But he went 1-for-2, knotting things up at 79 and sending it to overtime.

Then the Hokies turned up their intensity and outscored the Hawkeyes 14-9 in the final five minutes, which included a 7-for-8 mark from the stripe. Jaylen Curry led the way with nine points, while Butta Johnson and Isaiah Elohim had two apiece and Tyler Johnson added one.

“Certainly, a great sign here in November,” Tech head coach Mike Young said after the win. “Long way to go, as you know, but I take nothing away from our team. Iowa’s really good, exceptionally well-coached. Got good players.

“A lot to build upon, but we’ve got a long way to go and a lot more mountains to climb.”

Curry took over after halftime, scoring 23 of his 25 points in that stretch and making eight of his 14 shots. He put the Hokies on his back in overtime, getting to the rim for a layup to open the scoring, then banking in a three with two ticks on the shot clock to put them ahead 84-80 with 1:35 to play.

The sides traded buckets — two Elohim foul shots and a Curry jumper in the lane from Tech, with two Coleman layups for Iowa — before Tyler Johnson stepped up and knocked down a foul shot with 19 seconds on the clock to make it 89-85.

In nail-biting fashion, Coleman — who scored eight of his 20 points in overtime — had an and-one with 11.7 seconds remaining, cutting the margin to one. But Butta Johnson, Tech’s fifth-year senior, was clutch with two free throws to provide some breathing room at 91-88.

The Hawkeyes moved into the halfcourt, called timeout and set up a side out-of-bounds play with 5.8 seconds to go. They inbounded it to 7-foot-3 center Andrew McKeever, who passed it off to leading scorer Cooper Koch (26 points) for a 3-point look from the wing, but it was an airball.

The rebound fell in Curry’s hands, and he was fouled with 0.4 seconds remaining. His two foul shots iced the neutral-site victory, which should pay dividends for Tech’s résumé come March.

“We’re pretty damn hard to guard,” Young said. “And when you put [Amani] Hansberry into that mix, it’s a great connector of offenses. Elohim played a good ball game. Miles Heide did some things to help us. We’ve got to get more out of our five spot, and we will moving ahead. … But those guards were going. Elohim found some seams, so I look forward to seeing it on film.

I look forward to continuing to build with this bunch. They’re connected, they’re really enjoying playing with one another.”

The overtime finish overshadowed a back-and-forth affair that featured 57 fouls — 28 in the first half, including a technical on Young, his first in two years, and 50 in regulation — and 80 free throws.

It was the most fouls in a Tech game since 2015 (60 vs. West Virginia) and the most free throw attempts in a game since 2008 (83 at Georgia Tech).

Before Young’s technical at the 9:20 mark in the first half, the Hokies had been called for 10 fouls to the Hawkeyes’ four. It evened out after that, with a 22-20 foul margin towards Iowa the rest of the way.

That didn’t prevent Ben McCollum’s squad from attempting 47 foul shots, though — the most by a Tech foe since Jan. 30, 2005, when it lost 100-65 in its first trip to Cameron Indoor Stadium as an ACC member and Seth Greenberg was tossed as Duke took 49 free throws.

The Hokies had to manage foul trouble all evening in Sioux City, and they had five players with at least two personals at the half. Backup forward Musa Sagnia had three.

Yet, they battled, a charge spearheaded by Hammond. He was outstanding, with 15 points in the first half and 17 in the second for a new career high, surpassing his 30 from last season’s triple-overtime win over Virginia.

Hammond was 9-for-16 from the field, 3-for-5 from 3-point range, a perfect 11-for-11 from the stripe and added five rebounds and four assists without a turnover. The only other Tech player with that stat line — at least 32 points, five rebounds, four assists and 11 free throws — in a game since joining the ACC: Malcolm Delaney in 2011.

He made some magical plays, including his triple in traffic as the halftime buzzer sounded.

Through the first three games of the year, he’s averaging 22.7 points, 5.3 rebounds, 4.3 steals and 3.3 rebounds per game while shooting 48.8 percent from the floor, 42.9 percent from deep and 95 percent (19-for-20) from the line.

It was the third straight game that he, Curry and Hansberry all scored in double figures. Hansberry finished with 16 points (7-for-12), 12 of which came in the first half.

The other massive contributor was Elohim, who tallied 12 points and six rebounds. Ten of his points came in the first half.

The Hokies led for most of the first half — including by as many as eight — until an Isaia Howard trey with 2:33 pulled Iowa ahead for the first time at 35-34.

They jumped back in front with Hammond’s shot to end the half, and then his three free throws to begin the second after he was fouled shooting a 3-pointer.

But Howard — who was everywhere with 13 points, a game-high 15 rebounds and three steals — started to tilt things in the Hawkeyes’ direction. His second triple with 11:43 in the second half gave them a 58-56 lead, and his third with eight minutes on the clock — from way downtown — stretched the margin to five, Tech’s largest deficit of the game.

Iowa continued to attack favorable mismatches, particularly by getting Koch (5-for-11 FG, 15-for-17 FT) switched onto Hammond, and it provided fits, even when the Hokies doubled the post.

Tech willed its way back, though. Down 72-67 and with Heide having just fouled out, Hansberry ignited the team with a dunk. Then it started changing some things up defensively, and Hammond drilled a three off an offensive board from Tyler Johnson to tie it up at 72 with 4:35 left.

After Tate Sage knocked down the Hawkeyes’ fifth trey and provided a 78-76 lead with 1:24 to go, Curry blew through Iowa’s defense on his way to the rack for a layup. Then the Hokies got a stop, setting up a potential game-winning situation with 28.8 seconds remaining — 23 on the shot clock — coming out of Young’s timeout.

It was all Curry in that moment, and he got Iowa forward Cam Manyawu to switch onto him, hoisted a 3-pointer and drew a foul as the shot clock expired.

However, he missed the first two attempts — his only two misses on a 5-for-7 night from the stripe. Curry’s third time was the charm, giving Tech a 79-78 lead with 6.1 seconds to play, but Iowa drew the ensuing fifth foul on Hammond and Coleman made one of his two free throws to force overtime.

The Hawkeyes kept the door open for the Hokies in that moment and a few others, especially given their dominance on the glass. They won that battle 40-26, often facing an undersized Tech lineup with Hammond (5-11) and Curry (6-1).

How did the Hokies find a way to grind it out then?

“We’re pretty damn physical too,” Young said. “They’re big, needless to say. They struggled with our small lineup, and they did some matchup punting and tried to get one on Curry, who had fouls, and they took advantage of some things. But we fought. We fought, and we found a way out.

“We mixed up some coverages; I thought that helped us. Again, I want to look at it and continue to improve, put them in better spots as we move ahead.”

Tech returns home to Cassell Coliseum for its next two games. It hosts Mount St. Mary’s on Friday night before Richmond visits on Monday evening. Both are 7 p.m. ET tips on ACC Network Extra.

Final (OT): Virginia Tech 93, Iowa 88
